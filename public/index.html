Controls

Touch: put your left thumb down anywhere on the left half and drag. That's the stick: pull down to pull the nose up, sideways to roll. On the right: the throttle lever (the top band is afterburner), FIRE (the selected missile or bomb), GUN, WPN (cycles IR missile, radar missile and air-to-ground stores), LOCK (radar lock), TGT (next target), FLR and CHF. Drag on empty screen on the right to look around. The top-left buttons pause, change the view, set the map range, open the wingman radio, and work the gear, flaps, speedbrake and look-back. Hold your phone sideways.

Scenarios

Pick a mission on the main menu or build your own: up to 8 jets a side on four maps (Islands, Alpine, Desert Canyons, Arctic Fjords) at dawn, day, dusk or night, in clear skies to full overcast. Any seat can be a human seat or AI, so you can fly co-op against AI, team vs team online, or mix the two. Share a scenario with Copy link; online, the host's scenario is sent to everyone in the room.

AI pilots: press ⚙ on any slot in the builder to set its personality (balanced, aggressive, reckless, cautious, scared, BVR sniper, dogfighter, loyal wingman, lone wolf), its orders (hunt, patrol, escort the leader, radar-silent ambush, target drone), weapons rules, target priority, and sliders for reflexes, awareness, gunnery, flying, tactics, aggression, courage and teamwork. Scared pilots break off and run home when hurt or shot at; reckless ones never do. AI radio calls appear in the kill feed.

Your HUD shows every contact you know about: boxes are your own radar tracks, triangles are datalink tracks shared by your wingmen, and blue marks are friendlies. The game picks the target closest to your nose; press N to cycle. Night and haze shrink how far you can see.

Nations and jets

Each jet uses its real thrust, weight, wing, radar, sensors, gun and missiles. Nothing is balanced, so some jets are simply better. USA has the best radars and missiles, plus the F-22. Russia has big thrust-vectoring Flankers with IRST. China has AESA radars, the long-range PL-15 and the gunless J-20. Europe has agile deltas with the best jamming, missile warning and the ramjet Meteor.

Flying

Fly-by-wire: holding pull commands up to the jet's G limit, limited by angle of attack. Hard turns bleed speed. You turn tightest at corner speed, marked by the blue caret on the speed tape. Above it, use the airbrake (B) before a hard turn. Thrust-vectoring jets (F-22, Su-35, Su-57) keep nose authority at very low speed. Pulling above 7.5 G fills G-STRESS, and when it's full you black out.

Guns

Each jet has its own cannon. The M61 fires 100 rounds/s; the Russian GSh-30-1 carries only 150 heavy rounds; the Typhoon's BK-27 and the Rafale's 30M791 sit in between. The J-20 has no gun at all. Put the gun cross on the lead pipper; it's only effective inside about 1,000 m.

Sensors and stealth

Radar (the fan at the bottom) range depends on the radar and on the target's radar cross-section. Stealth jets are seen at a small fraction of normal range: an F-22 at about a tenth, a J-20 a little more, and the Su-57 about three-quarters. Jamming shortens enemy radar range further. Tab locks (STT), which gives radar missiles datalink updates but sets off the bandit's RWR. LPI radars (F-22, F-35-class AESAs) are hard for RWRs to hear.

IRST (Russian, Chinese and European jets, plus the Super Hornet) passively sees engine heat. It shows an amber circle with a bearing but no range, and it can cue IR missiles without warning the target. Terrain masks everything: a ridge between you blocks radar, IRST, missile seekers and your eyes. Hide behind mountains.

Stealth is about aspect. A stealth jet is smallest seen nose-on; from the side or tail it reflects several times more, and opening the weapon bays to fire lights it up for a moment. An F-22 nose-on is seen at roughly a tenth of normal range (about 5 km by an Su-35), but side-on at nearly twice that. Low-band early-warning radars see stealth jets much farther, though too coarsely to guide a missile. Eyes don't care about stealth: you can spot a jet visually at 5-9 km depending on its size and angle, but not in your blind spot behind and below.

Fly low. The curve of the earth hides a low jet from a distant ground radar: a SAM mast sees a jet at 50 m out to about 48 km, at 15 m only about 36 km. Close to the ground, radars also lose you in the ground return, gradually: a big SAM sees a jet at 100 m out to about 30 km, at 40 m only about 10 km, and treetop-high only inside 3 km. Short-range SAMs and ships are built for low flyers and see lower. Fighters looking down at you lose range, most when you're near the ground. Below about 70 m over land (120 m over water), radar missiles get confused by your reflection off the surface and can fly into the ground. Smooth sea is worst for them.

Radars filter out ground clutter, so a jet that is beaming (flying across your line of sight) with ground behind it drops into the notch. That means being below the radar, or under about 250 m, where the beam grazes the ground. Down low, the notch works even against ground radars and missiles at your height. Missile warning (MAWS) sees a burning motor within about 5 km and is fitted to the European, Chinese, Su-57 and F-22 jets. Without it, a silent missile only shows once its seeker goes active.

Missiles

Fox 2 (IR): AIM-9, R-73/74, PL-10, IRIS-T, MICA IR. Press 1 and hold the bandit in the seeker circle until the tone goes steady. Modern ones can lock far off the nose using a helmet sight (T), a radar lock or the IRST.

Fox 3 (active radar): AIM-120, R-77, PL-12/15, MICA EM, Meteor. Press 2; it needs a radar track. It's datalinked while you hold a lock, then goes active ("pitbull") near the target. The Meteor is ramjet-powered, so it keeps thrusting the whole way and is very hard to outrun. Fox 1 (R-27ER): semi-active, so you must keep your radar lock until impact (the HUD shows ILL), or it goes dumb.

The scale right of the altitude is the launch zone: IN RNG means it can hit a target holding course; SHOOT means no escape.

Defending

When a missile is guiding on you, the missile coach (a box low on the HUD; toggle it in the main menu) says what kind it is, where it is (clock position, range, time to impact), which radar you have to beat, and the best defence right now. BEAM markers on the heading tape show the two headings that put the threat on your wing.

Long range: drag. Turn cold (missile at your 6), afterburner, dive. The motor burns for only a few seconds; after that the missile coasts and slows, so a target running away makes it fly much farther. Before pitbull: cut the datalink. A Fox 3 is steered by the launcher's radar until it goes active. Beam the launcher and get below it to break the lock, and the missile flies to where you were. After pitbull: notch. Put the missile at your 3 or 9 o'clock and stay below it, so your closing speed on its seeker drops under ~30 m/s and the Doppler filter loses you. Then chaff (Z): it barely works unless you are beaming. Don't turn too early, or the missile just corrects. Fox 1: notch the launcher, which has to keep you locked. Last ditch (under 2-3 km): chaff and break hard across its path.

Against IR: you can't notch a heat-seeker. Get out of afterburner, pop flares (C) as it launches, and break hard across its path. Flares work best early and side-on. Or put a mountain between you. Practise both with Training 8 and 9.

Getting shot down

A missile hit usually tears the jet apart; gunfire tends to leave it burning and spinning in. Most of the time the pilot ejects. Watch for the chute.
